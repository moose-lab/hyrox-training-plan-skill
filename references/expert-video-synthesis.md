# Expert Video Synthesis and Use Policy

## Purpose

This reference converts selected professional videos into **bounded coaching inputs** for the HYROX skill. A video is not scientific proof. Use the transcript-derived material to improve practical exercise selection, sequencing, or athlete communication only after it is labelled as `COACH_PRACTICE` or `TRANSFER_EVIDENCE` and paired with its primary evidence boundary.

> **Rule:** Never promote a coach’s anecdote, race result, commercial protocol, or training volume into a universal hard constraint.

## Transcript-Derived Themes

### Training and specificity

Dan Plews presents a practical hierarchy in which training frequency and tolerable volume create the base for later intensity and race-specific work. Rich Ryan’s team emphasizes introducing Pro-level station demands gradually rather than changing every demanding station at once. These are sound programming heuristics only when individualized for baseline training history, movement skill, equipment access, available time, and recovery status. They complement, but do not replace, the small HYROX physiological study by Brandt et al., which found associations between performance, VO2max, and endurance-training volume in 11 recreational athletes.[1]

### Strength dose and concurrent trade-offs

WOD Science correctly cautions against assuming one strength dose fits everyone. The useful agent behavior is to identify whether the athlete’s limiting factor is likely station strength endurance, running capacity, movement efficiency, or recovery tolerance; then expose the weakest quality progressively. The agent must not claim that one strength session per week maintains or develops strength for every HYROX athlete, nor should it treat general strength-maintenance research as HYROX-direct evidence.

### Fueling and gut tolerance

WOD Science and Triage Nutrition both reinforce that carbohydrate strategy must be practiced, not improvised on race day. This aligns with the International Society of Sports Nutrition nutrient-timing position stand, which describes carbohydrate intake during extended high-intensity exercise as context dependent and emphasizes total daily nutrition and feeding distribution over a mythical narrow recovery window.[2] The agent must label all HYROX race-fueling quantities as **transfer evidence** unless direct HYROX trials exist.

### Sleep and recovery

The selected athlete-sleep interview is retained as a discovery source but has no usable transcript. Use the 2021 athlete-sleep consensus instead. The consensus supports individualized sleep screening and long-term sleep behavior, while warning against one-size-fits-all hours, simplistic wearable interpretation, and overconfident short-term performance claims.[3]

## Prompt Fragments for Agent Use

Use these as composable instruction fragments, not as athlete-facing medical advice.

1. **Evidence boundary:** “For every programming rationale, label it `HYROX_DIRECT`, `TRANSFER_EVIDENCE`, or `COACH_PRACTICE`; do not describe transfer evidence or coach practice as HYROX-proven.”
2. **Volume hierarchy:** “Build only as much weekly frequency and easy aerobic work as the athlete can recover from; add quality work after confirming the baseline is stable.”
3. **Station progression:** “Introduce a new race-load exposure to one or two station patterns at a time, then reassess soreness, technique, and subsequent running quality before adding another.”
4. **Fuel practice:** “Treat race fueling as a trained skill: test food, fluid, and carbohydrate options in relevant sessions; report tolerance observations rather than prescribing a universal protocol.”
5. **Sleep readiness:** “Use sleep, soreness, stress, and perceived readiness to initiate a coach-review flag and offer lower-impact alternatives; do not diagnose, cancel training automatically, or interpret consumer wearable scores as clinical results.”
6. **Supplement guardrail:** “Before educational supplement discussion, ask about age, medical conditions, medication, pregnancy, anti-doping obligations, prior tolerance, and qualified professional advice; do not make supplements required.”
